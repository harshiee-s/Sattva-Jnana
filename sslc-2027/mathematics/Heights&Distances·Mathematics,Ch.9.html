Some Applications of Trigonometry

Surveyors, architects, sailors and astronomers measure things they cannot reach: the height of a tower, the width of a river, the distance to a ship or a star. With one measured angle and one measured distance, the trigonometric ratios give the rest.

Section 1 · Heights and distances

Line of sight, elevation and depression

A student looks at the top of the Qutub Minar. Three things in the picture have special names.

Height of the minar CD = CB + BD, where BD = AE (the observer’s eye height) and BC = AB · tan θ.
Line of sight
The line drawn from the eye of an observer to the point on the object being viewed.
Angle of elevation
The angle between the line of sight and the horizontal when the object is above the horizontal level (we raise our head).
Angle of depression
The angle between the line of sight and the horizontal when the object is below the horizontal level (we lower our head).
Angle of elevation: the object is above the horizontal level, so we raise our head.
Angle of depression: the object is below the horizontal level, so we lower our head.
In simple wordsBoth angles are always measured from the horizontal line through the eye, never from the vertical. Look up: elevation. Look down: depression.
Key factIf A looks up at B with angle of elevation θ, then B looks down at A with angle of depression θ. The two horizontal lines are parallel, so these are alternate angles and are equal.

01What do we need to find the height of the minar?

  1. The distance DE from the student to the foot of the minar.
  2. The angle of elevation ∠BAC of the top of the minar.
  3. The height AE of the student (eye level).

Then CD = CB + BD, with BD = AE. In △ABC, BC is opposite the known angle A and AB is adjacent, so we use tan A = BCAB (or cot A). Solving gives BC; adding AE gives the height.

02Choosing the right ratio

You knowYou wantUse
Distance (adjacent)Height (opposite)tan θ = opp/adj
Height (opposite)Distance (adjacent)tan θ, or cot θ = adj/opp
Height (opposite)Slanted length: ladder, rope, string (hypotenuse)sin θ = opp/hyp
Ground distance (adjacent)Slanted length (hypotenuse)cos θ = adj/hyp
Make your own clinometerStick a protractor on a ruler, hang a thread with a small weight from its centre, and look along the ruler at the top of a tree. The thread hangs vertically, so (90° − reading) is the angle of elevation. Measure your distance from the tree with a tape and use height = distance × tan θ + eye height.
Section 2 · Solved examples

Solved examples

Every problem follows the same routine: draw a neat diagram, mark the right angle and the given angle, choose the ratio that contains one known and one unknown side, and solve.

E1A tower stands vertically on the ground. From a point on the ground 15 m from the foot of the tower, the angle of elevation of the top is 60°. Find the height of the tower.
AB is the tower; ∠ACB = 60°.
  1. Step 1 · Ratiotan 60° = ABBCtan links the opposite side (height) and the adjacent side (distance).
  2. Step 2 · Substitute√3 = AB15
  3. Step 3 · AnswerAB = 15√3 m ≈ 25.98 m
E2An electrician must repair a fault on a 5 m pole at a point 1.3 m below the top. What length of ladder, inclined at 60° to the horizontal, does she need, and how far from the foot of the pole should she place it? (√3 = 1.73)
BD = 5 − 1.3 = 3.7 m; BC is the ladder.
  1. Step 1 · Height to reachBD = 5 − 1.3 = 3.7 m
  2. Step 2 · Ladder (hypotenuse) → sinBDBC = sin 60° ⟹ BC = 3.7 × 2√3 ≈ 4.27 m
  3. Step 3 · Foot of ladder → cotDCBD = cot 60° = 1√3 ⟹ DC = 3.7√3 ≈ 2.14 m
E3An observer 1.5 m tall is 28.5 m from a chimney. The angle of elevation of the top of the chimney from her eyes is 45°. Find the height of the chimney.
AB = AE + EB, with EB = CD = 1.5 m.
  1. Step 1 · Ratiotan 45° = AEDE ⟹ 1 = AE28.5 ⟹ AE = 28.5 m
  2. Step 2 · Add eye heightAB = 28.5 + 1.5 = 30 m
E4From a point P on the ground, the angle of elevation of the top of a 10 m building is 30°. A flag on top of the building makes the angle of elevation of the top of the flagstaff 45°. Find the length of the flagstaff and the distance of the building from P. (√3 = 1.732)
AB is the building, BD the flagstaff.
  1. Step 1 · Triangle PABtan 30° = ABAP ⟹ 1√3 = 10AP ⟹ AP = 10√3 ≈ 17.32 m
  2. Step 2 · Triangle PADtan 45° = 10 + x10√3 = 1 ⟹ x = 10(√3 − 1) ≈ 7.32 m
  3. AnswerFlagstaff ≈ 7.32 m; distance ≈ 17.32 m.
E5The shadow of a tower is 40 m longer when the Sun’s altitude is 30° than when it is 60°. Find the height of the tower.
BC = shadow at 60°; DB = shadow at 30°.
  1. Step 1 · Triangle ABCtan 60° = hx ⟹ h = x√3 (1)
  2. Step 2 · Triangle ABDtan 30° = hx + 40 ⟹ 1√3 = hx + 40 (2)
  3. Step 3 · Solve(x√3)√3 = x + 40 ⟹ 3x = x + 40 ⟹ x = 20
  4. Answerh = 20√3 m ≈ 34.64 m
E6The angles of depression of the top and bottom of an 8 m building from the top of a multi-storeyed building are 30° and 45°. Find the height of the multi-storeyed building and the distance between the two buildings.
PC is the tall building, AB the 8 m building.
  1. Step 1 · Alternate angles∠PBD = 30° and ∠PAC = 45° (PQ ∥ BD ∥ AC).
  2. Step 2 · Triangle PBDPDBD = tan 30° ⟹ BD = PD√3
  3. Step 3 · Triangle PACPCAC = tan 45° = 1 ⟹ PC = AC
  4. Step 4 · CombinePD + 8 = PD√3 ⟹ PD = 8√3 − 1 = 4(√3 + 1) m
  5. AnswerPC = 4(3 + √3) ≈ 18.93 m; AC = 4(3 + √3) ≈ 18.93 m
E7From a point on a bridge across a river, the angles of depression of the banks on opposite sides are 30° and 45°. If the bridge is 3 m above the banks, find the width of the river.
AB is the width of the river; PD = 3 m.
  1. Step 1 · Triangle APDtan 30° = PDAD ⟹ AD = 3√3 m
  2. Step 2 · Triangle PBD∠B = 45° ⟹ BD = PD = 3 m
  3. AnswerAB = 3 + 3√3 = 3(√3 + 1) m ≈ 8.20 m
Section 3 · Problems with two angles

Problems with two angles

Many problems give two angles of elevation from two points on the same line (a shadow that grows, a boy who walks, a balloon that drifts). Two right triangles share the same height, which gives two equations.

Handy formulaIf the angles of elevation of the top of a height h from two points d apart on the same side are α and β (α < β), then
h = d · tan α · tan βtan β − tan α
It comes from h = x tan β and h = (x + d) tan α. In exams, show the two equations; use the formula to check.

Section 4 · Exercise solutions

Exercise solutions

All fifteen questions solved with diagrams where they help. Try each one first.

Q1A circus artist climbs a 20 m rope stretched from the top of a vertical pole to the ground. The rope makes 30° with the ground. Find the height of the pole.
AC is the rope.
  1. Ratiosin 30° = ABAC ⟹ 12 = AB20
  2. AnswerAB = 10 m
Q2A tree breaks in a storm; the broken part bends so that its top touches the ground at 30°, 8 m from the foot. Find the height of the tree.
AB is the standing part, AC the broken part.
  1. Standing parttan 30° = AB8 ⟹ AB = 8√3 m
  2. Broken partcos 30° = 8AC ⟹ AC = 16√3 m
  3. AnswerHeight = 8√3 + 16√3 = 24√3 = 8√3 m ≈ 13.86 m
Q3Two slides: one 1.5 m high inclined at 30°, the other 3 m high inclined at 60°. Find the length of each slide.
  1. Small slidesin 30° = 1.5L₁ ⟹ L₁ = 3 m
  2. Big slidesin 60° = 3L₂ ⟹ L₂ = 6√3 = 2√3 m ≈ 3.46 m
Q4The angle of elevation of the top of a tower from a point 30 m from its foot is 30°. Find the height of the tower.
  1. Ratiotan 30° = h30 ⟹ h = 30√3
  2. Answerh = 10√3 m ≈ 17.32 m
Q5A kite flies at a height of 60 m. Its string makes 60° with the ground. Find the length of the string (no slack).
  1. Ratiosin 60° = 60L ⟹ L = 120√3
  2. AnswerL = 40√3 m ≈ 69.28 m
Q6A 1.5 m tall boy stands some distance from a 30 m building. The angle of elevation from his eyes to the top increases from 30° to 60° as he walks towards it. Find the distance he walked.
Height above eye level = 30 − 1.5 = 28.5 m.
  1. Height above eyes30 − 1.5 = 28.5 m
  2. First distance28.5tan 30° = 28.5√3
  3. Second distance28.5tan 60° = 28.5√3
  4. Answer28.5√3 − 28.5√3 = 57√3 = 19√3 m ≈ 32.91 m
Q7From a point on the ground, the angles of elevation of the bottom and top of a transmission tower on a 20 m building are 45° and 60°. Find the height of the tower.
  1. Distancetan 45° = 20/d ⟹ d = 20 m
  2. Total heighttan 60° = (20 + h)/20 ⟹ 20 + h = 20√3
  3. Answerh = 20(√3 − 1) m ≈ 14.64 m
Q8A 1.6 m statue stands on a pedestal. From a point on the ground, the angles of elevation of the top of the statue and of the pedestal are 60° and 45°. Find the height of the pedestal.
  1. Pedestal htan 45° = h/d ⟹ d = h
  2. Statue toptan 60° = (h + 1.6)/h ⟹ h√3 = h + 1.6
  3. Answerh = 1.6√3 − 1 = 0.8(√3 + 1) m ≈ 2.19 m
Q9The angle of elevation of the top of a building from the foot of a tower is 30°, and of the top of the tower from the foot of the building is 60°. The tower is 50 m high. Find the height of the building.
  1. Distance between themtan 60° = 50d ⟹ d = 50√3
  2. Buildingtan 30° = hd ⟹ h = 50√3 × 1√3
  3. Answerh = 503 = 16⅔ m ≈ 16.67 m
Q10Two equal poles stand on opposite sides of an 80 m road. From a point between them, the angles of elevation of their tops are 60° and 30°. Find the height of the poles and the distances of the point from them.
The point O is x m from one pole.
  1. Pole 1h = x tan 60° = x√3
  2. Pole 2h = (80 − x) tan 30° = 80 − x√3
  3. Solve3x = 80 − x ⟹ x = 20
  4. Answerh = 20√3 m ≈ 34.64 m; distances 20 m and 60 m
Q11A TV tower stands on a canal bank. From the opposite bank the angle of elevation of its top is 60°; from a point 20 m further back it is 30°. Find the height of the tower and the width of the canal.
  1. From Ch = x√3 (x = width)
  2. From Dh = x + 20√3
  3. Solve3x = x + 20 ⟹ x = 10 m
  4. Answerh = 10√3 m ≈ 17.32 m; width = 10 m
Q12From the top of a 7 m building, the angle of elevation of the top of a cable tower is 60° and the angle of depression of its foot is 45°. Find the height of the tower.
The horizontal from the building top splits the tower at 7 m.
  1. Distancetan 45° = 7/d ⟹ d = 7 m
  2. Upper part7 × tan 60° = 7√3 m
  3. AnswerHeight = 7 + 7√3 = 7(√3 + 1) m ≈ 19.12 m
Q13From the top of a 75 m lighthouse, the angles of depression of two ships, one behind the other, are 30° and 45°. Find the distance between the ships.
  1. Near ship75/tan 45° = 75 m
  2. Far ship75/tan 30° = 75√3 m
  3. Answer75√3 − 75 = 75(√3 − 1) m ≈ 54.90 m
Q14A 1.2 m girl sees a balloon moving horizontally at a height of 88.2 m. The angle of elevation changes from 60° to 30°. Find the distance travelled by the balloon.
  1. Height above eyes88.2 − 1.2 = 87 m
  2. At 60°87/√3 = 29√3 m
  3. At 30°87√3 m
  4. Answer87√3 − 29√3 = 58√3 m ≈ 100.46 m
Q15A man on top of a tower sees a car approaching at an angle of depression of 30°. Six seconds later it is 60°. Find the time the car takes to reach the foot of the tower from this point.
  1. First positiond₁ = h/tan 30° = h√3
  2. Second positiond₂ = h/tan 60° = h/√3
  3. Distance in 6 sh√3 − h√3 = 2h√3
  4. Speed2h6√3 = h3√3 per second
  5. Answertime = d₂ ÷ speed = h√3 × 3√3h = 3 seconds

Section 5 · Quick revision

Quick revision

  1. The line of sight is the line from the eye of an observer to the point viewed.
  2. The angle of elevation is between the line of sight and the horizontal when the object is above the horizontal level.
  3. The angle of depression is between the line of sight and the horizontal when the object is below the horizontal level.
  4. Heights, lengths and distances between distant objects can be found using trigonometric ratios.

02Common mistakes

Watch out forMeasuring the angle from the vertical instead of the horizontal · forgetting to add the observer’s eye height · putting the angle of depression at the bottom instead of at the top (use alternate angles) · using sin when the hypotenuse is not involved · rationalising wrongly, e.g. 8/(√3 − 1) = 4(√3 + 1).

03Exam tips

Always draw and label a diagram, write the ratio in words first (tan θ = opposite/adjacent), keep √3 exact until the last line, then give the approximate value if asked.

Section 6 · Practice

Practice questions

Solve on paper first, then open the answer.

P1From a point 20 m from the foot of a tree, the angle of elevation of its top is 45°. Find its height.

20 × tan 45° = 20 m.

P2A ladder 10 m long makes 60° with the ground. How high up the wall does it reach?

10 sin 60° = 5√3 ≈ 8.66 m.

P3A pole casts a shadow equal to √3 times its height. Find the Sun’s altitude.

tan θ = h/(h√3) = 1/√3, so θ = 30°.

P4From the top of a 50 m cliff, the angle of depression of a boat is 30°. How far is the boat from the foot?

50/tan 30° = 50√3 ≈ 86.6 m.

P5A kite string is 100 m long and makes 30° with the ground. Find the height of the kite.

100 sin 30° = 50 m.

P6The angles of elevation of a tower top from two points 10 m apart are 45° and 60°. Find the height.

h = 10·tan45·tan60/(tan60 − tan45) = 10√3/(√3 − 1) = 5(3 + √3) ≈ 23.66 m.
